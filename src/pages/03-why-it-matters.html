Why it matters

Banks, credit unions and health systems depend on the same providers and the same third-party software. When one of them fails, many institutions feel it at once.

Critical infrastructure

The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) lists Financial Services and Healthcare and Public Health among the nation's critical infrastructure sectors.1 Online banking, payments, patient portals and claims all depend on internet-facing services working securely.

One failure, many institutions

Recent events show how far a single failure or compromise can spread:

  1. February 2024A ransomware attack on Change Healthcare, a single claims-processing vendor, affected approximately 192.7 million individuals.2
  2. March 2024Malicious code was found in XZ Utils, a library included in major Linux distributions.3
  3. July 2024A faulty CrowdStrike software update affected about 8.5 million Windows devices.4
  4. September 2025A self-replicating worm compromised more than 500 packages in the npm software registry.5

Smaller institutions

Large banks and health systems have dedicated teams for this work. Community banks, credit unions and rural hospitals face the same risks with small IT staffs. SISL's public research and open-source software are written with them in mind and are free to use.
